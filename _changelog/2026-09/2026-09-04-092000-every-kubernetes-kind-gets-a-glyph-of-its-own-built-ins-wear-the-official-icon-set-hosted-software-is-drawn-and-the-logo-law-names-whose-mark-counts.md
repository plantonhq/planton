# Every Kubernetes kind gets a glyph of its own, the built-in objects wear the official Kubernetes icon set, everything hosted on Kubernetes is Planton-drawn, and the logo law names whose mark counts

## What changed

- **All 123 Kubernetes logos are judged under the logo law.** Before, eight Istio kinds wore one hand-copied sail, cert-manager and its three certificate kinds one mark, External Secrets and its three store kinds another, the two Percona operators one Percona mark, and six more pairs shared a file -- 28 kinds sharing a glyph and 120 naming no provenance. Six files (Postgres, MongoDB, Jenkins, Keycloak, SigNoz, Solr) embedded raster images of 74 to 264 KB that every console downloaded for a 34 px icon. Fifteen were hand-drawn imitations of other projects' trademarks (a Trino bunny, an Istio sail, RabbitMQ's ears, Percona's mark) -- the one thing a trademark owner objects to. The built-in objects that were right (Deployment, Service, Namespace...) were the official Kubernetes icons in their *labeled* variant, three-letter text inside the frame that dies at 18 px. Now no two kinds share bytes, every file opens with its provenance, no file embeds a raster, and no file imitates anyone's mark.
- **The seventeen built-in objects wear the official Kubernetes Icons Set, unlabeled.** Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, Job, CronJob, Service, Ingress, ConfigMap, Secret, PersistentVolumeClaim, StorageClass, ServiceAccount, RBAC (the role binding), NetworkPolicy, ResourceQuota, HorizontalPodAutoscaler, and Namespace are copied from `kubernetes/community/icons/svg/resources/unlabeled/`, which the Kubernetes community publishes for architecture diagrams under Apache-2.0 or CC-BY-4.0: a filled Kubernetes-blue heptagon with a white pictogram, legible at 18 px on both washes. Each file's `<desc>` names the source file and the license.
- **The fifteen built-ins the set does not cover are Planton-drawn on the set's own base**, so the seven-sided frame keeps one meaning on every sheet: "an object Kubernetes itself defines". The set's copyright license permits the derivative; the `<desc>` says so. A pod disruption budget is three pod bars over the minimum-available floor line, a priority class stacked chevrons rising, a raw manifest a document with a bracket pair, a Gateway two pillars under a lintel with the way through them open, a GatewayClass the same gate dashed, the Gateway API definitions a document carrying the gate, a ListenerSet the gate with its ports lit, the ingress controller the gate with its gear at the threshold, an HTTPRoute a web page with its arrow, a gRPCRoute a call arrow and its reply, a TCPRoute a plug in its socket, a TLSRoute a padlock, a UDPRoute datagrams flying loose, a ReferenceGrant a key handed across the wall between namespaces, a BackendTLSPolicy a padlock in front of the backend server.
- **Everything hosted on Kubernetes -- 88 kinds of other projects' software, their children, and their operators -- is Planton-drawn with no frame**, in Kubernetes blue (`#326ce5`, with `#74a5f4` as the second tone, settled at 18 px on both washes; a lighter `#8fb5f5` washed out on the light plinth), from one vocabulary: a relational store is a cylinder with table rows and a relational cluster two cylinders behind one proxy line, a columnar store a cylinder sliced into standing columns, a document store a stack of pages, a graph store nodes joined by their relationships, a vector store vectors fanning from a query point, a cache a key, a search index a lens over the cylinder it searches and a document index a lens over the document, an object store a pail, a secrets vault a door with a dial, an authorization store a padlock over tuple rows; a stream platform a bundle of partitioned pipes and a topic one pipe with its partitions, a stream user a person beside the pipe, a connector cluster a plug and a connector its cable into the pipe, a mirror one pipe reflected into another, a schema registry a brace pair, a stream console a screen of pipes, a pub/sub broker one publisher fanning out to three subscribers, a queue broker an exchange feeding three queue boxes; a stream job a gear on its pipe, a compute cluster a head node with workers on spokes, a workflow scheduler a directed graph with a clock at its start, a query engine a lens over a grid of tables, a data explorer a screen of charts, a notebook server a page of cells beside its spiral, an experiment tracker a run curve with its points, a load generator a swarm converging on one target, a workflow engine an hourglass on a timeline, a build server a gear with a play mark, a delivery controller a commit delivered into the cluster box, chained steps handing to the next, pipeline segments with their checks, an identity provider a person holding their key, a registry containers on a shelf, a chart release a sealed package with its tag; a dashboard a screen with a chart, logs label ticks with uneven lines, traces spans stepping down a waterfall, an observability backend a heartbeat pulse, a metrics stack a dial gauge over its series, a collector signals funneled into a pipe; a mesh a woven lattice and its definitions a document carrying it, an authorization policy a shield with a check, peer authentication a shield with a handshake, request authentication a shield with a key, a destination rule a signpost, a proxy filter a nozzle in the traffic line, a service entry a door set into the mesh, telemetry a signal point with its arcs, a network fabric three planes linked by one datapath, a DNS writer a globe with the record it writes; a provisioner a server rising on an arrow, a node pool a rack, a node class a server's blueprint, an event-driven autoscaler a queue with the scale arrows it drives, a cluster autoscaler a rack with plus and minus, a metrics server a ruler, an admission controller a fence with its shield, a policy engine a clipboard of checked rules, a backup tool a box with its return arrow, a runner scale set a row of runner chips; a certificate a sealed sheet with ribbons, an issuer a stamp and a cluster issuer the stamp over the bar that spans the cluster, an external secret a locked sheet drawn in by an arrow, a secret store a cabinet of drawers and a cluster store the cabinet over the bar. **Every operator draws the thing it reconciles inside a reconcile loop** -- the columns, the row cylinder, the stack of pages, the paired cylinders, the lens over the cylinder or the document, the exchange and queues, the partitioned pipes, the gear on the pipe, the head node and workers, the partitioned blocks, the person with their key, the funnel, the pipeline segments, the certificate, the locked sheet, the runner chip -- so fourteen operators differ in their primary form and share the loop as their family.
- **The logo law names whose mark counts.** It said a kind that IS a provider product wears the provider's official mark where the provider offers its icons. Kubernetes's catalog is mostly other people's software, and the law had no sentence for that. It now does: a kind wears an official mark only when it is a product or built-in object of the provider it belongs to; software of another project hosted on a provider is not the provider's product and is always Planton-drawn, whatever that project's own terms would allow -- so every catalog logo stays under one owner's terms, free of the attribution, linking, and no-recolor obligations project marks carry onto every surface that shows them. Two more clauses: a drawn glyph never contains, modifies, or *resembles* a provider's or a project's mark (an obviously derived drawing is the misuse its owner pursues); and where a provider publishes its own icon set under a license that permits derivatives, drawn glyphs for that provider's own objects may extend the set on its base. Stated in `architecture/catalog-kind.md`, the `pkg/cataloglogo` package comment, forge step `021-logo` (whose provider list now carries Kubernetes and whose vocabulary carries the hosted-software concepts above, so the next provider draws its hosted kinds from the same list in its own palette), and the update flow's replacement clause. The gate's `missing-provenance` failure text names every provenance form it accepts.
- **Kubernetes joins the judged-provider pin.** `judgedProviders` is `gcp, cloudflare, kubernetes`; its 148 baseline entries are gone and nothing else in the baseline moved. Copying one Kubernetes glyph onto a sibling fails the gate and the pin at both kinds by name.

## Why

On a diagram the icon is the one identity signal nothing else supplies, and a Kubernetes chart -- the one most customer charts are -- said it in borrowed marks, shared marks, and imitations. Reading the trademark terms of forty projects, one by one, and carrying their attribution notices onto every console page forever is a debt the law should not take on to save drawing forty glyphs; the provider's own products wear its marks, and everything else says what it is. The built-in objects are the exception that proves the rule: Kubernetes publishes its resource icons for exactly this, under a license that lets the set be extended, so the objects Kubernetes defines wear Kubernetes's own icons and the frame comes to mean "a Kubernetes object" on every sheet.

## How to check

```bash
md5 -q catalog/kubernetes/*/logo.svg | sort | uniq -d          # prints only the Planton brand tile's hash (the law's one intended sharing)
grep -L '<desc>' catalog/kubernetes/*/logo.svg                 # prints nothing: every file names its provenance
grep -l '<image\|base64' catalog/kubernetes/*/logo.svg         # prints nothing: no embedded raster
go test ./pkg/cataloglogo/ ./pkg/anatomy/                      # green, Kubernetes absent from the baseline and pinned
go run ./tools/catalog-logo-sheet/ -provider kubernetes        # then open the HTML it names
```

## What comes next

- The logos publish to the CDN at their versionless keys with the next release tag; consoles pick them up after the cache turns over (four hours at most), with no platform change.
- AWS, Azure, DigitalOcean, Auth0, and OpenFGA are judged the same way as their catalog work reaches them: the provider's own products wear its marks where offered, everything hosted on it is drawn from the shared vocabulary, the sheet at 18 px on both washes, then the judged-provider list gains a string.
