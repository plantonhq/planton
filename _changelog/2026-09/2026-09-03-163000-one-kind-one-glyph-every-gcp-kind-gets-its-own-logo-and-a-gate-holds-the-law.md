# One kind, one glyph — every GCP kind gets a logo of its own, Planton's kinds wear Planton's mark, and a gate holds the law for every provider

## What changed

- **The logo law, written once and enforced.** A kind's `logo.svg` is the glyph every console card, catalog row, wizard header, and diagram node wears, and on a diagram it is the one identity signal nothing else supplies: color says the family, shape says the nature, only the glyph says WHICH thing. The law: one kind, one glyph. A kind that IS a provider product wears the provider's official mark, unaltered. Planton's own kinds wear the Planton brand mark. Every other kind — a database inside an instance, a job of a service, a record in a zone, a grant, a schedule — wears a Planton-drawn glyph that says what that kind is, in the provider's icon language, never containing or modifying a provider mark. Two kinds of one provider never share a glyph, except Planton's own kinds sharing Planton's. When a provider later publishes an official icon for a kind wearing a drawn glyph, the official one replaces it. Stated in `architecture/catalog-kind.md`, taught by the new forge step `021-logo`, carried into the update flow as a replacement rule, pointed at by the audit rule.
- **All 99 GCP logos rebuilt under the law** (closes #520, and more than it asked). 49 kinds wore one generic hexagon and five wore one generic network mark; among the kinds the issue counted as fine, the Cloud SQL instance, its database, and its user shared one glyph, so did the GKE cluster and its node pool, and the DNS zone and its record — exactly the picture a chart built from one product's family must never show. Now: 41 kinds that are Google products wear Google's official flat product icon from the official library (one vector each, about 1 KB, crisp at every size; AlloyDB from the 2025 set, the only place its mark exists), 57 kinds wear Planton-drawn concept glyphs in Google's blue palette on the 24-grid (a cylinder for a database, a person for a user, a key for a key, a bell for an alert policy, a funnel for a sink, a target for an SLO, a clock badge for a schedule, a person badge for a grant), and the runner wears the Planton brand mark. Every one of the 99 was judged on a contact sheet at 24 px and 48 px on both a light and a dark plinth wash before it was kept; nothing hand-drawn before was presumed good, and every previous drawing was redrawn to the same standard.
- **Planton's own kinds wear the official Planton mark on every provider.** The runner on AWS, Azure, GCP, and Kubernetes and the Kubernetes Planton Platform and Planton Operator carry one byte-identical file: the console's canonical P in white on Planton's black rounded tile with a faint light keyline — one file that reads on light and dark backgrounds, because a catalog logo is served from a CDN into an image element and cannot follow a theme switch. Their invented drawings retire.
- **Provenance lives in the file.** Every logo opens with a `<desc>` naming its class: `Official Google Cloud product icon: <library>/<file>`, `Planton-drawn glyph: <what it depicts>`, or `Planton brand mark`. The update flow greps the drawn ones when an official icon appears; the gate reads the class to know which sharing is intended; the next reader learns why a drawing looks the way it does. No sources table to keep in sync.
- **The `pkg/cataloglogo` gate grows two rules beside its parse check.** `shared-glyph`: two kinds of one provider wearing byte-identical logo content is a violation at each kind, naming the others and the fix in the law's words, unless every wearer is the Planton brand mark. `missing-provenance`: a logo whose `<desc>` names no class. Both carry a burn-down baseline in `pkg/anatomy`'s exact shape (`pkg/cataloglogo/baseline.yaml`, entries `<path>:<rule>`, regenerated only with `PLANTON_REGEN_LOGO_BASELINE=1`, removals only); `malformed-svg` never does. GCP enters with zero entries and a dedicated test pins that it stays there; AWS, Azure, Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, Kubernetes, Auth0, and OpenFGA enter with today's sharing and missing provenance recorded (918 entries), so the gate is green on merge and every provider pays its list down as its logo set is judged. The CI lane header (`lint.catalog-logo.yaml`) says all of this.

## Why

A Spanner instance, a Bigtable instance, and a Firestore database inside a data room were told apart by label alone; a Cloud Run service, a Compute Engine VM, and an Eventarc trigger wore the same mark; and a chart made of a Cloud SQL instance, its database, and its user was three identical icons in a row. Google's own architecture diagrams give every product a glyph and every part of a product a glyph of its own, and a reader who knows those glyphs reads a diagram without labels. The catalog already held about forty hand-drawn vectors that did this well — the practice existed and was never written down, so it was applied unevenly and the placeholder shipped forty-nine times. Writing the law where the forge and update flows read it, and putting a gate behind it, is what stops the fiftieth.

## How to check

```bash
cd catalog/gcp && for d in gcp*/; do echo "$(md5 -q $d/logo.svg) ${d%/}"; done | sort | uniq -c -w 32 | sort -rn | head -3
go test ./pkg/cataloglogo/ ./pkg/anatomy/
```

The first shows no GCP hash shared by two kinds; the second is green with GCP absent from the baseline.

## What comes next

- The logos publish to the CDN at their versionless keys with the next release tag; consoles pick them up after the cache turns over, with no platform change.
- The remaining providers' logo sets are judged glyph by glyph as their catalog work reaches them, each under the same law and the same 24 px / 48 px contact-sheet check; `pkg/cataloglogo/baseline.yaml` is the authoritative list of what each provider still owes, and it shrinks to zero.
