# Every Cloudflare kind gets a glyph of its own, the logo law learns what to do when a provider keeps its marks, and a contact-sheet tool judges glyphs at the sizes the console draws them

## What changed

- **All 66 Cloudflare logos are Planton-drawn under the logo law.** Before, 34 Cloudflare kinds wore one shield-with-arrow glyph (every Zero Trust, device, Gateway, certificate, and watching-layer kind among them), six zone kinds wore one DNS glyph, the three load-balancer kinds one, the three email kinds one, the Access group and policy one -- 48 kinds sharing a glyph and all 66 naming no provenance -- so a Zero Trust chart was a row of identical shields told apart by label alone. Now every kind wears a glyph that says what it is, drawn from one primitive vocabulary so the set reads as one language: a zone is a globe (its settings a globe with a gear, its TLS posture a globe with a padlock, a customer's hostname a globe with a name plate), a record is one row of a table pointing at its answer, every certificate is a sealed sheet that differs in its primary form (an upload arrow for material you bring, the origin server behind an Origin CA sheet, two sheets for mTLS, a pull arrow leaving an origin-pull certificate), a Worker is a bolt, a snippet braces around a bolt, a Workflow three chained steps, Pages a browser window, KV a box of key-value rows and a pair one key beside its value, D1 a cylinder with a table grid, R2 a pail, Hyperdrive a bolt through a database, a queue messages in line along a pipe, the secrets store a safe and a secret a keyhole, the AI gateway a gate with a spark, an API token a ticket with a key on it, Logpush log lines leaving a sheet, a notification policy a bell and its webhook a hook, web analytics a browser with a bar chart, the Access application a door whose keyhole is a shield, a policy a shield with a check, a group two people, an identity provider an ID card, a service token a key with a gear, an infrastructure target a server in a crosshair, the organization a building, the tunnel an arch, a route an address on a path, a virtual network three nodes in a triangle, a DNS location a pin on a network, a Gateway policy a gate with a shield and its settings a gate with a gear, the Zero Trust list a ruled column with a shield, the device profiles a laptop (bare, with a person on its screen, with a check on its screen), the MCP portal a doorway with a spark and the MCP server a server with one. No two share bytes; every file opens with `<desc>Planton-drawn glyph: …</desc>`.
- **The drawn set wears Cloudflare's orange, not its dashboard blue.** The files the catalog shipped before were Cloudflare's docs icons recolored to `#0051c3`, and on the console's dark theme that blue on the dark plinth is a smudge -- at the 18 px an attachment plate draws, invisible. Both tones were rendered side by side at 18 px on a dark wash; Cloudflare's brand orange (`#f6821f`, with `#fbad41` as the second tone) reads on both themes and is the tone the set wears. Recorded in the forge rule so the next drawn set for this provider starts there.
- **The logo law learns what to do when a provider keeps its marks.** The law said a kind that IS a provider product wears the provider's official mark. That presumed every provider offers its icons for third-party use. Google Cloud does, explicitly. Cloudflare does not: its trademark guidelines permit its marks in word form only and reserve its logos and icons for written permission, and its developer-docs repository's license excludes them. So the law now reads: the official mark where the provider offers its icons for third-party diagrams; where it does not, every kind of that provider wears a Planton-drawn glyph and no file under that provider names the `Official` class. Stated in `architecture/catalog-kind.md`, the `pkg/cataloglogo` package comment, forge step `021-logo` (which now carries a per-provider list -- Google Cloud offers, Cloudflare reserves, any new provider is read before a single kind is classified), and the update flow's replacement clause.
- **Glyphs are judged at the sizes the console draws them.** The console draws a card's icon at 34 px on its plinth, a chip's or globe's at 26 px, a vault door's at 22 px, and an attachment plate's at 18 px -- so 18 px is the size that decides, and the earlier "24 px and 48 px" was a guess. The forge rule now says 18 px, says a corner badge alone does not separate two glyphs at that size (kinds of one family share a base shape and differ in the primary form), and names the tool below.
- **`tools/catalog-logo-sheet` renders the contact sheet.** `go run ./tools/catalog-logo-sheet/ -provider cloudflare` walks the kind registry the way the gate does and writes one HTML page with every logo of the provider at 18, 26, 34, and 48 px on a light and a dark wash, the kind folder under each, as `<img>` elements -- the way the console renders a logo, so a glyph that leans on a stylesheet fails here as it would in the product. A report, not a gate, in the shape of `tools/catalog-page-census`. The last two providers judged their sheets with a script that lived in `/tmp` and is gone; this one is committed.
- **The gate's judged-provider pin is one table-driven test.** `TestJudgedProviderLogoSets` runs over `judgedProviders = []string{"gcp", "cloudflare"}`: no violation under the provider's directory, no entry for it in `baseline.yaml`. A provider joins the list in the change that empties its entries; the next provider adds one string. Cloudflare's 114 baseline entries are gone and nothing else in the baseline moved.

## Why

On a diagram the icon is the one identity signal nothing else supplies -- color says the family, shape says the nature, only the glyph says which thing -- and half of Cloudflare's cards said it in one picture. The law that fixed GCP had a gap the first non-Google provider exposed: it assumed the provider's marks were ours to use. Reading Cloudflare's terms rather than assuming them is what the law now asks of every provider, and drawing all 66 is what the answer required. Judging at 18 px rather than 24 is what the console actually renders; a set that passes at 24 and vanishes on a plate is not judged.

## How to check

```bash
md5 -q catalog/cloudflare/*/logo.svg | sort | uniq -d          # prints nothing: no two Cloudflare kinds share a glyph
grep -L '<desc>Planton-drawn glyph:' catalog/cloudflare/*/logo.svg   # prints nothing: every file names its provenance
go test ./pkg/cataloglogo/ ./pkg/anatomy/                      # green, Cloudflare absent from the baseline
go run ./tools/catalog-logo-sheet/ -provider cloudflare        # then open the HTML it names
```

## What comes next

- The logos publish to the CDN at their versionless keys with the next release tag; consoles pick them up after the cache turns over (four hours at most), with no platform change.
- AWS, Azure, Kubernetes, DigitalOcean, Auth0, and OpenFGA are judged the same way as their catalog work reaches them: read the provider's terms first, then the sheet at 18 px on both washes, then the judged-provider list gains a string.
