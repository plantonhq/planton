# A GKE maintenance exclusion held until end of support plans a change on every apply

**Area**: `catalog/gcp/gcpgkecluster` (both engines: `iac/tf/main.tf` maintenance_exclusion, `iac/pulumi/module/cluster.go` maintenance exclusions)
**Type**: bug (a perpetual diff; no harm observed, but it hides real drift in every preview)

## What a platform engineer sees

A cluster declares an exclusion the way GKE requires -- a `start_time`, an `end_time`, and `end_time_behavior: UNTIL_END_OF_SUPPORT`:

```yaml
maintenancePolicy:
  exclusions:
    - exclusionName: hold-node-upgrades
      startTime: "2026-09-08T00:00:00Z"
      endTime: "2027-03-06T00:00:00Z"
      scope: NO_MINOR_OR_NODE_UPGRADES
      endTimeBehavior: UNTIL_END_OF_SUPPORT
```

Every preview and every update of the stored cluster then shows the same change, with nothing edited:

```
~ maintenance_policy = [{... "maintenance_exclusion":[{"end_time":"", ... "end_time_behavior":"UNTIL_END_OF_SUPPORT" ...}] ...}]
                    -> [{... "maintenance_exclusion":[{"end_time":"2027-03-06T00:00:00Z", ...}] ...}]
```

GKE stores no end time for an exclusion held until end of support and reads `end_time` back empty; the module sends the declared date again. Seen 2026-10-04 on two estate clusters (a `create-stack-job --operation preview` of each, OpenTofu engine), before an unrelated change; both applied without error, as every earlier apply had.

## Why it matters

A preview is how an operator proves a cluster has no drift before changing it. A diff that is always there trains people to read past the one line in the preview that should stop them.

## Suggested fix

When `end_time_behavior` is `UNTIL_END_OF_SUPPORT`, send no `end_time` (or ignore its difference) on both engines, and say in the field comment that the end date is GKE's to hold. Prove it with a preview of a live cluster reading no change.
